## The message

> Hey — I need a favour, and it's the kind that involves telling me my app is annoying.
>
> I built **Varsity Companion** for a hackathon: a study app for SA university students. Every deadline in one place — assignments *and* bursary applications — a study plan that tells you what to work on first, and a materials finder that works on a weak connection.
>
> It's a **demo**, not a finished product. I need 10 people to use it for a week and tell me the **first thing that annoyed them**. Not "nice app". The annoying part. That's the whole reason I'm asking.
>
> **https://uni-companion.scrapymog.workers.dev**
>
> Three things to know:
> 1. It's a demo — rough edges are expected, and finding them is the point.
> 2. Sign up with an email you'll remember. Password recovery is handled by me personally (no email sending yet) — message me if you get locked out and I'll sort it in a minute.
> 3. Use **Chrome or Brave**. Voice dictation only works in some browsers — if you don't see a mic, that's expected, just type.
>
> Whatever annoys you first, message it to me. Even "I don't understand this screen" is genuinely useful.

## Why it's written this way

- **"Tell me the first thing that annoyed you"** beats "any feedback?" — people give honest answers when you make it clear the criticism is the gift.
- **"Not 'nice app'"** removes the polite flattery that makes feedback useless.
- **Saying it's a demo** first stops them judging it as a finished product.
- **Naming the mic limitation** stops someone reporting it as a bug you must fix.
- **"Even 'I don't understand this' is useful"** gives them permission to be confused, which is where the real problems live.

## Who to pick

People who will **reply**, not people who will sign up and vanish. If you get 10 replies, you have 10 real problems. If you get 50 signups and no replies, you have nothing.

Include **at least two people from a different degree** — a Humanities or Law student will find problems your Computer Science friends never see.

## What to do with the replies

Send them to me as they come. I'll turn them into a fix list. Group them like this and they become a roadmap:

- **Broken** — something that does not work
- **Confusing** — works, but they could not find it or understand it
- **Missing** — something they expected and it wasn't there

The first category is a bug. The second is a design problem, and it is usually worth more than any feature you could build this week.
